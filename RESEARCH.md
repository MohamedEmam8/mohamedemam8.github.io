# Research & Expertise

## Research Vision

My research aims to translate advanced biomechanics technology into clinical practice. By combining markerless motion capture, statistical analysis, and clinical evaluation, I investigate how the body moves during rehabilitation, aging, and disease—and how we can optimize recovery.

**Core question:** How can we use objective, accessible motion analysis to improve rehabilitation outcomes?

---

## Research Focus Areas

### 1. Gait Analysis & Biomechanics

Understanding how people walk—and how walking changes with age, injury, or neurological disease—is fundamental to rehabilitation practice.

**Key topics:**
- Kinematic patterns in aging and functional decline
- Eccentric vs. concentric exercise effects on gait mechanics
- Gait adaptations in stroke and neurological conditions
- Walking efficiency and energy cost in elderly populations
- Muscle activation patterns during gait (sEMG)

**Relevance:** Objective gait analysis guides rehabilitation targeting and measures treatment success beyond simple walking speed.

---

### 2. Markerless Motion Capture (OpenCap)

OpenCap is a game-changer in biomechanics—it brings lab-grade motion analysis to clinical settings without expensive marker systems.

**Why this matters:**
- **Accessibility:** Smartphone or tablet camera captures movement in real-time
- **Scalability:** Can be used in clinics, homes, or research labs
- **Clinical relevance:** Patients see their own movement patterns instantly
- **Research power:** Captures detailed kinematic data for statistical analysis

**My expertise:**
- OpenCap system setup, calibration, and clinical implementation
- Data processing and quality assurance workflows
- Integration with Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM) for group analysis
- Video-based motion capture for rehabilitation assessment

**Current application:** Analyzing gait kinematics in elderly populations undergoing resistance training.

---

### 3. Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM1D)

SPM1D is a powerful statistical framework for analyzing movement data across groups—going beyond simple joint angle averages to identify *when and where* movement patterns differ.

**Advantage over conventional analysis:**
- Analyzes entire kinematic curves (not just peak values)
- Accounts for temporal variability in movement patterns
- Identifies clinically meaningful kinematic differences
- More statistically rigorous for small-sample studies

**Applications:**
- Comparing gait patterns between diseased and healthy populations
- Evaluating intervention effects on movement quality
- Identifying disease-specific kinematic signatures
- Kinematic patterns in muscular dystrophy (manuscript under review)

---

### 4. Neurorehabilitation & Stroke Recovery

Stroke often leaves people with gait abnormalities, balance deficits, and reduced independence. My research explores evidence-based interventions.

**Research domains:**
- **Electrical Stimulation:** Russian current and other modalities for postural control, balance, fall risk reduction
- **Proprioceptive Training:** Effectiveness for headache, postural stability, functional recovery
- **Gait-Specific Interventions:** Movement pattern retraining post-stroke
- **Fall Risk Reduction:** Balance assessment and prevention strategies for elderly stroke survivors

**Publications:**
- Meta-analysis: Electrical stimulation for gait & postural control (2025)
- RCT: Russian current therapy for post-stroke balance (2026)
- Systematic review: sEMG biofeedback for dysphagia (under review)

---

### 5. Aging Physiology & Functional Decline

How does aging change the way we move, and what can we do about it?

**Key investigations:**
- Resistance training in older adults: eccentric vs. concentric effects
- Blood flow restriction exercise protocols for elderly strength & hypertrophy
- Functional mobility assessment and intervention
- Preventing age-related loss of independence

**Relevance:** By understanding age-related movement changes, we can design targeted interventions to keep people active and independent.

---

### 6. Musculoskeletal Conditions

Beyond gait, I investigate biomechanics of common musculoskeletal problems.

**Research areas:**
- **Cervicogenic Headache:** Cervical proprioception, muscle activation, postural control
- **Neck Pain:** Wrist/forearm muscle performance in students with chronic nonspecific neck pain
- **Spinal Conditions:** Maitland & Mulligan mobilisation effects

**Focus:** Understanding the biomechanical underpinnings helps inform clinical management.

## PhD Research

### Focus
**Postural control and proprioceptive rehabilitation in cervicogenic headache** — surface EMG, posturography and machine-learning analysis of biomechanical data — and **resistance training on the cost of walking and muscle architecture in healthy older adults**, combining metabolic cost measurement, B-mode ultrasound of muscle architecture, and markerless motion capture with statistical parametric mapping. Supervisors: Prof. Tibor Hortobágyi and Assoc. Prof. Magda Ramadan. **Joint PhD, 2022–2026.**

### Postural control and proprioceptive rehabilitation in cervicogenic headache
Originating in my MSc and continuing through the doctorate, this strand uses **surface EMG, posturography (centre-of-pressure) and machine-learning analysis of biomechanical data** to ask who benefits from proprioceptive rehabilitation and why. It has produced four clinical trials and a machine-learning classification study (*Journal of Clinical Medicine* 2024, *BMC Neurology* 2025, *Physiology International* 2026, *Life* 2026) and remains an active interest.

### Emerging direction (beyond the PhD)
Alongside the doctorate, I have built a markerless motion-capture and SPM research programme — including a muscular-dystrophy gait study (manuscript under review) and post-stroke gait machine-learning work. This is the methodological foundation of my proposed postdoctoral research.

### Muscular Dystrophy Gait Study (manuscript under review)
Muscular dystrophy causes progressive muscle weakness, leading to compensatory gait patterns and functional decline. Using markerless motion capture (OpenCap) and Statistical Parametric Mapping, we identify disease-specific kinematic signatures that may:
- Provide objective biomarkers for disease progression
- Detect early functional decline
- Guide rehabilitation targeting
- Measure intervention efficacy

### Methodology
- **Participants:** Individuals with muscular dystrophy vs. age-matched controls
- **Assessment:** Overground walking, captured via OpenCap (smartphone-based)
- **Analysis:** SPM1D for kinematic curves comparing groups
- **Outcomes:** Identification of kinematically distinct movement patterns

## Pilot & Active Projects

### 1. Eccentric vs. Concentric Overload Resistance Training (RCT)

**Question:** How do different resistance training types affect gait biomechanics in older adults?

**Design:** Randomised controlled trial
**Participants:** Older adults (60+ years)
**Intervention:** 
- Eccentric-focused overload training
- Concentric-focused overload training
- Control group

**Measurements:**
- Gait kinematics (OpenCap)
- Sit-to-stand kinematics (OpenCap)
- Functional mobility
- Strength (isokinetic dynamometry)

**Analysis:** Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM1D)

**Relevance:** Guides exercise prescription for maintaining gait quality and functional independence in aging.

---

### 2. Electrical Stimulation for Gait & Postural Control (Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis)

**Status:** Published 2025 (*Physiology International*)

**Scope:** Comprehensive review of electrical stimulation modalities (Russian current, NMES, TENS) for gait and postural control post-stroke

**Key findings:**
- Multiple stimulation types show promise for balance improvement
- Evidence quality varies; further RCTs needed
- Russian current emerging as effective for anterior tibial group activation

**Clinical value:** Systematizes evidence to guide clinical decision-making.

---

### 3. Russian Current Therapy for Post-Stroke Balance (RCT)

**Status:** Published 2026 (*Neurological Sciences*)

**Question:** Does targeted Russian current stimulation of the anterior tibial group reduce fall risk post-stroke?

**Design:** Randomised controlled trial
**Participants:** Post-stroke patients with balance deficits
**Intervention:** Russian current stimulation (anterior tibial) vs. control
**Outcomes:** Balance, fall risk, gait velocity, functional mobility

**Result:** Positive effect on balance and fall risk reduction.

## Methodology Arsenal

### Motion Capture
- **OpenCap (Markerless):** Gold-standard for accessible, real-time gait analysis
- **Camera setup:** Smartphone/tablet, minimal calibration
- **Output:** 3D skeleton animation + kinematic data export

### Statistical Analysis
- **SPM1D:** Statistical Parametric Mapping for curve analysis
- **Linear Mixed-Effects Models:** Complex study designs with repeated measures
- **MATLAB, Python, R:** Custom analysis scripts and visualization

### Clinical Assessment Tools
- **Isokinetic Dynamometry:** Quantify muscle strength (ankle, knee, hip)
- **Surface EMG (sEMG):** Muscle activation patterns during movement
- **Functional Tests:** Timed Up and Go, Sit-to-Stand, walking speed
- **Balance Assessment:** Force plate (if available), postural sway

### Imaging & Morphometry
- **MRI Muscle Segmentation:** Quantify muscle volume and fat infiltration
- **Image Analysis:** MATLAB/Python for segmentation and measurement

### Study Designs
- **Randomised Controlled Trials (RCTs):** Gold-standard evidence
- **Systematic Reviews & Meta-Analyses:** Synthesizing literature
- **Scoping Reviews:** Mapping emerging evidence
- **Pilot Studies:** Feasibility and method development

## Why Motion Capture & Biomechanics Matter

Most clinicians rely on observation and simple functional tests. While valuable, these lack objectivity and precision. Markerless motion capture changes that—giving us:

✓ **Objective metrics** of movement quality  
✓ **Early detection** of functional decline  
✓ **Evidence-based rehabilitation targeting**  
✓ **Measurable outcomes** for treatment efficacy  
✓ **Accessibility** — can be deployed in any setting  

This is the future of rehabilitation: data-driven, personalized, and grounded in biomechanics.
