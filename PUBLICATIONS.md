#### 10. Effect of Different Types of Electrical Stimulation on Postural Control and Gait After Stroke: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
**Citation:** *Physiology International*, 2025;112(4)  
**DOI/Link:** [https://akjournals.com/view/journals/2060/112/4/article-p393.xml](https://akjournals.com/view/journals/2060/112/4/article-p393.xml)

**Study Type:** Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  
**Relevance:** Stroke Rehabilitation, Electrical Stimulation, Gait & Balance  
**Impact Factor:** Q2 Journal

**Summary:** Comprehensive review synthesizing evidence on electrical stimulation modalities (NMES, Russian current, TENS, etc.) for post-stroke gait and postural control recovery. Meta-analysis examines effect sizes across modalities.

**Key Findings:**
- Multiple stimulation types show promise for improving balance
- Russian current emerging as particularly effective for anterior tibial activation
- Heterogeneity in protocols limits direct comparisons
- High-quality RCTs needed to establish optimal parameters

**Clinical Value:** Guides evidence-based selection of electrical stimulation for stroke rehabilitation.

#### 11. Exploring Blood Flow Restriction Exercise Protocols for Elderly Populations: A Scoping Review of Cuff Pressure, Frequency, and Duration for Muscle Strength, Hypertrophy, and Functional Abilities Outcomes
**Citation:** *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, 2025;14(12):4185  
**DOI/Link:** [https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/12/4185](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/12/4185)

**Study Type:** Scoping Review  
**Relevance:** Aging Physiology, Exercise Physiology, Strength Training  
**Impact Factor:** Q1 Journal (MDPI)

**Summary:** Maps the landscape of blood flow restriction (BFR) exercise literature in elderly populations, examining how cuff pressure, training frequency, and session duration affect strength, muscle hypertrophy, and functional outcomes.

**Key Insights:**
- BFR shows promise for low-load strength gain in elderly
- Optimal cuff pressure and protocol parameters still emerging
- Limited high-quality RCTs in true elderly populations
- Potential for accessible strength training in those with mobility limitations

#### 12. Proprioceptive Training Improves Postural Stability and Reduces Pain in Cervicogenic Headache Patients: A Randomized Clinical Trial
**Citation:** *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, 2024;13(22):6777  
**DOI/Link:** [https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/22/6777](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/22/6777)

**Study Type:** Randomised Clinical Trial  
**Relevance:** Musculoskeletal, Neurorehabilitation, Proprioception  
**Impact Factor:** Q1 Journal (MDPI)

**Summary:** First study to comprehensively evaluate proprioceptive training (balance and coordination exercises) for cervicogenic headache, measuring pain reduction, postural stability, and functional capacity.

**Key Results:**
- Proprioceptive training significantly reduces headache intensity and frequency
- Postural stability (single-leg stance, tandem walking) improves substantially
- Functional cervical movements improve
- Effect maintained at 3-month follow-up

**Impact:** Provides non-pharmacological, evidence-based alternative for cervicogenic headache management.
